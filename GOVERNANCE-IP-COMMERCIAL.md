# Governance, IP, and Commercial Opportunity

## Current position

The mission comes first: design Ana for girls who have the least access to capable technology.

Commercial applications may emerge if the technology proves broadly useful. Those applications are not inherently in conflict with the mission.

## Principle

> Commercial success should help fund and expand the mission rather than redirect the product away from its intended users.

## Possible future structure

One model to evaluate later:

- A mission-governed Project Ana entity protects the educational purpose, safety principles, and girls-first design.
- A commercial entity or licensees develop applications for other markets.
- Licensing or commercial returns support mission deployments.

This is a hypothesis, not a current organizational decision.

## IP

Do not prematurely optimize for patents or ownership percentages.

Priorities:

1. Keep clear records of who contributes what.
2. Establish written agreements before substantial collaborative engineering begins.
3. Treat meaningful contributors fairly.
4. Understand third-party and open-source licenses before incorporating technology.
5. Protect the ability to pursue the mission.
6. Decide what should be open, licensed, trademarked, patented, or kept proprietary only after the product architecture is clearer.

## Danny's role

Danny's role is primarily to convene human and financial capital around a validated problem:

- education expertise
- community expertise
- AI/software expertise
- mechanical/electrical engineering
- industrial design
- child safety and ethics
- philanthropy and investment

The project should not depend on Danny presenting himself as the expert in domains where other collaborators have deeper expertise.
