# Ana Meeting with Anandana

**Date:** 2026-08-10  
**Primary objective:** Determine whether the problem is real and important enough to pursue together.

The meeting is not intended to finalize hardware, business structure, or the model.

## 1. Return to the original observation

A useful opening:

> I keep thinking about what you told me: in some households where technology is scarce, the best device may stay with the adults, and girls may end up with the least capable device or none at all. It struck me that this could be another obstacle to education.

Then listen.

## 2. Validate the problem

Questions:

- How common is this?
- Where have you or your mother observed it?
- Is the pattern different for girls and boys?
- Is device access a significant educational barrier or a secondary one?
- What other barriers are more important?
- Would personally owning a device change anything?
- What am I misunderstanding about girls' education in rural India?

## 3. Test the Ana hypothesis

Current hypothesis:

> Ana is a private, offline AI companion that belongs to one girl and exists to help her think, learn, and grow.

Explain briefly:

- personally owned
- works without internet
- voice-first
- private one-ear audio
- local model and knowledge library
- designed to build agency, not simply provide answers

Ask:

> Does this solve a meaningful problem, or are we solving the wrong problem?

## 4. Educational vision

Discuss:

- What should a girl gain after a year with Ana?
- What should Ana teach directly?
- When should Ana ask questions instead?
- What local curriculum and knowledge matter?
- What languages matter first?
- How should safety and sensitive topics be handled?
- How could girls participate in designing Ana?

## 5. Roles

Danny's proposed role:

> I don't see myself as the education expert. My role is to bring together the right people in education, AI, hardware, design, and philanthropy and help turn the strongest version of the idea into something testable.

Discuss whether Anandana and her mother want to shape the educational mission and field-research process.

## 6. Next 30 days

Agree on a small number of actions.

Possible next steps:

1. Anandana and her mother document what they know about the device-access problem.
2. Identify 5–10 girls, educators, or families for structured discovery interviews.
3. Identify one technical lead for a feasibility prototype.
4. Draft a one-page research protocol.
5. Reconvene after discovery rather than immediately committing to a product design.

## Three questions not to miss

1. **If Ana existed tomorrow, what would make you say, "Girls need this"?**
2. **What is my biggest misunderstanding about this problem?**
3. **Who else should be in this conversation?**

## Commercial / IP if it comes up

> If this becomes broadly useful, there may be commercial applications. I would like to structure those opportunities so they strengthen the mission rather than pull the project away from the girls it was designed to serve. I also want meaningful contributors to be treated fairly. I don't think we need to decide ownership or corporate structure before we know whether the problem and solution are real.
