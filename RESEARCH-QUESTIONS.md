# Research Questions

## Primary hypothesis

Girls in some low-resource households have less control over capable digital devices than adults and possibly boys, creating an additional barrier to educational opportunity.

This must be validated rather than assumed.

## Core questions

### Household access
- What devices exist in the household?
- Who owns each device?
- Who controls when each device can be used?
- Which device is most capable?
- Who gets priority?
- Does gender affect access?
- Does age affect access?
- Does schoolwork receive priority?

### Educational effect
- What learning activities are impossible or difficult because of device access?
- How often is learning interrupted by sharing?
- Does lack of privacy limit questions girls are willing to ask?
- Would ownership change frequency or type of use?

### Ana concept
- Would a girl carry Ana every day?
- Would she consider it hers?
- Would adults permit that ownership?
- Would other household members appropriate it?
- Does a voice-only interface work?
- Is one-ear private audio desirable?
- What topics would girls actually ask about?

### Cultural and language fit
- Which language should Ana speak?
- How common is code-switching?
- Which voice characteristics feel trustworthy?
- Which design choices feel childish, desirable, embarrassing, expensive, or inappropriate?
- What local curriculum and knowledge matter most?

## Key product question

**Does personally owning a private offline AI companion solve a meaningful educational problem that shared phones, tablets, and classroom hubs do not?**
