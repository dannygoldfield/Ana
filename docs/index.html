It’s for girls!

Ana is a private, offline AI thinking companion designed first for girls who have the least access to capable technology.

Guiding question: How do we put a trustworthy thinking companion into the hands of every girl, regardless of whether she has internet access?

The problem

In some households where capable technology is scarce, the best device may remain with adults. Children may receive older or less capable devices, and girls may have the least access or none at all. This may create another obstacle to education.

This is a hypothesis to be tested with girls, families, and educators rather than assumed.

The idea

Give one girl one private, capable, personally owned thinking companion.

  • Designed first for girls with limited access to capable technology
  • Personally owned rather than shared
  • Fully usable without an internet connection
  • Private by design
  • Built to support learning, curiosity, judgment, and agency

Ownership of thought

The device belongs to one person. Her questions, ideas, notes, and learning remain local unless she deliberately exports them. Ana should help her think rather than train her to defer to technology.

Ana One

The first prototype is a pocket-sized, voice-first device intended to prove the experience before optimizing manufacturing.

  • Push-to-talk interaction
  • Permanently attached, durable single earbud
  • No internet required for normal use
  • No cloud account required
  • Local memory
  • Rechargeable battery

What “local AI” means

The language model runs on Ana itself. Speech recognition, speech output, the educational knowledge library, conversations, and notes also remain on the device. Normal use does not depend on remote AI servers.

Privacy

The current concept uses a permanently attached single earbud rather than a loudspeaker. Private listening is built into the physical design.

Research question

Does personally owning a private offline AI companion solve a meaningful educational problem that shared phones, tablets, and classroom hubs do not?

What we need to learn

  • How household device access is allocated
  • Whether gender affects access and priority
  • Whether limited access materially affects learning
  • Whether privacy changes what girls are willing to ask
  • Whether girls would carry Ana and consider it theirs
  • Which languages, voices, curricula, and cultural choices matter first

Origin

Ana emerged from a conversation with Anandana, a filmmaker and academic in India who has worked with her mother, a professor in India, on efforts related to improving educational opportunities for girls in rural communities.

Her observation about unequal access to capable household technology led to a simple design question:

What if, instead of waiting for a girl to inherit yesterday’s technology, we designed a capable device specifically for her?
