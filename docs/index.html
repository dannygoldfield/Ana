A trustworthy thinking companion for every girl.

Ana is a private, offline AI thinking companion designed first for girls who have the least access to capable technology.

How do we put a trustworthy thinking companion into the hands of every girl, regardless of whether she has internet access?

The problem

Access to a device is not the same as access to technology.

In some households where capable technology is scarce, the best device may remain with adults. Children may receive older or less capable devices, and girls may have the least access or none at all.

This has not yet been established as a general fact. It is the hypothesis motivating our next stage of research.

A girl may not control access to the household's best device.
The idea

Give one girl one private, capable, personally owned thinking companion.

Ana is meant to belong to one girl. It should work without the internet, without a cloud account, without advertising, and without depending on a family phone.

Ownership of thought

Her questions. Her ideas. Her learning.

The device belongs to one person. Her questions, ideas, notes, and learning remain local unless she deliberately exports them.

Ana should help her think rather than train her to defer to technology.

Ana One

The first hardware prototype.

Ana One is currently defined as a pocket-sized, voice-first device intended to prove the experience before optimizing manufacturing.

What “local AI” means

The language model runs on Ana itself. Speech recognition, speech output, the educational knowledge library, conversations, and notes also remain on the device. Normal use does not depend on remote AI servers.

Privacy as a physical design choice

The current concept uses a permanently attached, durable single earbud rather than a loudspeaker. Private listening is built into the object rather than added as a setting.

Research

We are testing the problem before locking the solution.

The central product question is:

Does personally owning a private offline AI companion solve a meaningful educational problem that shared phones, tablets, and classroom hubs do not?

What we need to learn

  • How household device access is actually allocated.
  • Whether gender affects access and priority.
  • Whether limited access materially affects learning.
  • Whether privacy changes what girls are willing to ask.
  • Whether girls would carry Ana and consider it theirs.
  • Which languages, voices, curricula, and cultural choices matter first.
Origin

The project began with a conversation.

Ana emerged from a conversation with Anandana, a filmmaker and academic in India who has worked with her mother, a professor in India, on efforts related to improving educational opportunities for girls in rural communities.

Her observation about unequal access to capable household technology led to a simple design question:

What if, instead of waiting for a girl to inherit yesterday's technology, we designed a capable device specifically for her?
